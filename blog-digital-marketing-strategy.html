What Makes A Good Digital Marketing Strategy?

How your website, content, social media, advertising and customer journey can work together to create more effective digital marketing.

By Jay Perrin
← Back to blogs

10th September 2026 • Digital Marketing • UK Small Business

Digital marketing can quickly become complicated.

There are websites, Google, social media, SEO, email marketing, Meta Ads, Google Ads, content, reviews and dozens of other things businesses are constantly told they should be doing.

The result is that many businesses end up doing a little bit of everything without really knowing how any of it fits together.

A good digital marketing strategy helps solve that problem.

Rather than treating each marketing channel as something separate, a strategy looks at the bigger picture. It considers who you are trying to reach, what you want them to do and how each part of your marketing can help move them towards becoming a customer.

Start with a clear goal

Before choosing marketing platforms, content ideas or advertising budgets, it is important to understand what you actually want your marketing to achieve.

That might sound obvious, but it is surprisingly common for businesses to start marketing without a clear goal.

Depending on the business, your goal might be:

  • Generating more enquiries
  • Increasing online sales
  • Getting more bookings
  • Building awareness locally
  • Launching a new service
  • Growing an email list
  • Encouraging repeat business

Once you know what the main objective is, it becomes much easier to decide which marketing activities are actually worth your time and money.

Understand who you are trying to reach

Good marketing starts with understanding the people you want as customers.

You do not necessarily need a complicated customer persona document. You simply need a good understanding of who your customers are, what they care about and what usually causes them to start looking for a business like yours.

Think about things such as:

  • Where your customers are based
  • What problems they are trying to solve
  • What questions they commonly ask
  • What might make them hesitate
  • What makes them choose one business over another
  • Where they are likely to look for information

Understanding this makes every part of your marketing easier, from writing website copy to choosing advertising audiences.

Make sure your website supports the strategy

For most businesses, the website sits at the centre of digital marketing.

Somebody might discover your business through Google, social media, an advert or even a recommendation, but there is a good chance they will eventually visit your website before making a decision.

That means your website needs to do more than simply exist.

It should clearly explain what you offer, build trust and make it easy for visitors to take the next step.

Strong websites usually include:

  • Clear messaging
  • Easy navigation
  • Strong calls to action
  • Customer reviews
  • Examples of previous work
  • Useful information about your services
  • Simple contact or enquiry options

There is little value in paying to send more people to a website if the website itself does not encourage them to take action.

Choose the right marketing channels

One of the biggest mistakes businesses make is assuming they need to be active on every marketing platform.

They do not.

The right platforms depend on your business and your customers.

A local tradesperson might benefit heavily from Google, local search and Facebook. A professional B2B business might find LinkedIn far more useful. A highly visual consumer brand may naturally suit Instagram.

The aim is not to be everywhere.

It is to be visible in the places where your potential customers are most likely to discover and research businesses like yours.

Use content to support the customer journey

Content is often treated as something businesses need to produce simply because they have been told to post regularly.

A better approach is to create content with a purpose.

Different content can help customers at different stages of their journey.

For example:

  • Helpful guides can introduce people to your expertise
  • Case studies can demonstrate results
  • Reviews can build trust
  • Behind-the-scenes content can show the people behind the business
  • FAQs can remove common concerns
  • Product or service content can help people understand what you offer

Instead of asking, "What should we post this week?" try asking, "What would our potential customers find useful?"

That simple change usually leads to much better content.

Combine organic and paid marketing

Organic marketing and paid advertising are often discussed as though businesses need to choose between them.

In reality, they often work best together.

Your website, social media, reviews and content help build credibility over time. Paid advertising can then help put that business in front of more of the right people.

For example, somebody might first discover your business through a Facebook or Instagram advert.

They may then visit your website, check your Google reviews, look through your social media and finally decide to send an enquiry.

The advert started the journey, but the rest of your digital presence helped complete it.

Think about what happens after the click

A good digital marketing strategy does not stop when somebody clicks an advert or visits your website.

You also need to consider what happens next.

If someone lands on a service page, is it obvious how they can enquire? If they complete a form, what happens afterwards? If they request a quote, how quickly do they receive a response?

Small improvements to the customer journey can make a big difference to the results your marketing generates.

Sometimes the issue is not getting more traffic. It is making better use of the traffic you already have.

Build trust throughout your marketing

Trust plays a huge role in digital marketing.

Potential customers are often comparing several businesses before deciding who to contact.

Strong trust signals can make that decision much easier.

These can include:

  • Google reviews
  • Customer testimonials
  • Case studies
  • Real photographs
  • Qualifications or accreditations
  • Examples of previous work
  • Clear contact information

These elements should not be hidden away. They should appear naturally throughout your website, social media and advertising.

Keep your messaging consistent

Customers rarely interact with a business in just one place.

They may see an advert, visit your Instagram account, check your website and then find your Google profile.

Ideally, the message they receive at each stage should feel consistent.

Your visual branding, tone of voice, offers and overall positioning should all feel connected.

Consistency helps people recognise your business and builds familiarity over time.

Measure the things that actually matter

Digital marketing gives businesses access to huge amounts of data.

That can be useful, but it can also become distracting.

Not every number needs to be treated as equally important.

Likes, impressions, clicks and website visitors can all provide useful information, but ultimately the most valuable metrics are the ones linked to your business goals.

Depending on the campaign, that might include:

  • Enquiries
  • Sales
  • Bookings
  • Cost per lead
  • Conversion rate
  • Return on advertising spend

Good reporting should help you make better decisions, not simply produce more numbers.

Give your strategy time to work

Marketing rarely produces perfect results immediately.

Some campaigns need testing. Websites need refining. Advertising audiences and creative may need adjusting. Content takes time to build momentum.

A good strategy gives you a direction while still allowing room to learn.

Look at what is working, identify what is not and make sensible improvements rather than constantly starting again.

Do not be afraid to simplify

Sometimes the best digital marketing strategy is simpler than you think.

A small local business might not need six social media platforms, complicated automation and dozens of advertising campaigns.

It may simply need:

  • A strong website
  • A well-maintained Google Business Profile
  • A steady stream of customer reviews
  • Useful social media content
  • A focused advertising campaign

Doing a few important things consistently is often far more effective than trying to do everything.

A good strategy connects everything together

Ultimately, a good digital marketing strategy is not about finding one magical platform or tactic.

It is about creating a connected system.

Your advertising creates awareness. Your website explains what you do. Your reviews build trust. Your content demonstrates expertise. Your contact process makes it easy for somebody to become a customer.

Each part supports the others.

When those pieces work together, your marketing becomes much stronger than any individual channel could be on its own.

Final thoughts

There is no single digital marketing strategy that works for every business.

The right approach depends on your customers, your goals, your budget and the way people typically buy from you.

The key is to start with a clear objective, focus on the channels that make sense for your audience and build a joined-up customer journey around them.

For small businesses in Manchester, Stockport and across the UK, a clear and focused strategy can make digital marketing much easier to manage and far more effective.
