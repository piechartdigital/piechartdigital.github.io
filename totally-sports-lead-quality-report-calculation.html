Totally Sports Insurance

How lead quality is calculated

Each Meta lead gets a score out of 10. The sport and phone number carry the most weight because they indicate how well the enquiry fits the sports we prioritise and whether the number is in a UK format suitable for a callback.

Individual lead scores

Sport and phone number each contribute up to 4 points. First name contributes up to 2 points.

Sport

  • 4 points for motocross or MX, motorsport, rugby, mountain biking or MTB, downhill, and enduro. These are the report’s Tier 1 sports.
  • 2 points for boxing, kickboxing, MMA, American football, cycling, rock climbing, football, ice hockey, BMX, skateboarding, tennis, swimming, wrestling, surfing, snowboarding, skiing, Gaelic football or lacrosse. Recognised variants such as “footy” and “mixed martial arts” also qualify.
  • 1 point for an unlisted answer of one or two words that passes the basic text checks.
  • 0 points for blank or junk entries, or unrecognised answers of three or more words.

The same sport rules apply across campaigns. For example, football earns 2 points even when the lead came from an MTB ad.

Phone number

  • 4 points for standard UK mobile and 01/02 landline formats. The report accepts a leading 0 or +44.
  • 2 points for other plausible UK number formats.
  • 0 points for non-UK numbers, missing or invalid entries, and obvious dummy numbers.

The phone check assesses the number’s format only.

First name

  • 2 points for a first name on the reference list or a recognised spelling variant.
  • 1 point for an unlisted first name that passes the basic checks.
  • 0 points for missing or junk entries, or when the full name contains letters outside A–Z, including accented letters. Spaces and common name punctuation are allowed.

When a lead loses a point

The report adds the three field scores. If at least two fields each score 1 point or less, it subtracts 1 from the total, with a minimum of zero.

For example, 4 points for phone, 1 for sport and 1 for first name give a total of 6 before the deduction and 5/10 after it.

The three headline scores

For each period, the report adds the final scores of all qualifying leads and divides by the number of leads. Each lead counts equally.

  • Yesterday covers the previous UK calendar day.
  • Running seven days covers the seven UK calendar days ending yesterday.
  • Last week covers the most recently completed Saturday-to-Friday reporting week.

The report uses the original Meta lead emails in Gmail. Website enquiries are excluded. A period with no qualifying leads shows a dash.

The seven-day campaign table

The table groups the running seven-day leads by campaign. Tier 1 sports shows the percentage of each campaign’s leads that earned all 4 sport points, rounded to a whole percentage.

Each campaign’s quality score starts with the average of its individual lead scores. If the campaign has at least 4 leads and fewer than 50% earned all 4 sport points, the report subtracts 1 point from the average before rounding. The minimum is zero. This adjustment applies only to the campaign table.

For example, a campaign with 8 leads and 3 Tier 1 sport answers shows 38% in the Tier 1 sports column. If the individual leads total 69 points, the average is 8.625. After the deduction and rounding, the campaign displays 7.6/10.

Rounding and colours

Average scores round to one decimal place. Whole scores display without a trailing zero, such as 8/10. The colours follow the displayed score.
